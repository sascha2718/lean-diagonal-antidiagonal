# Hausdorff dimension of planar box-like self-affine sets with rotations

Lean 4 formalisation of the paper *Hausdorff dimension of planar box-like self-affine sets with
rotations* by Demi Allen, Antti Käenmäki, R. Dániel Prokaj, Károly Simon and Sascha Troscheit
(2026). The paper is a working manuscript; the arXiv identifier and the Palomar entry are added
when they exist.

## Scope

A planar box-like self-affine set with rotations is generated by affine maps whose linear parts
are diagonal or anti-diagonal, with both types present. For such a set `X` that is proximal and
satisfies the projective exponential separation condition, the library proves that the
coordinate projections of the equilibrium state at the affinity dimension are
double-dimensional and exact-dimensional precisely at and above an explicit threshold, that
`dim_H X = dimaff(X)` when `dimaff(X) < 2` under either an entropy condition or the strong open
set condition, that the projected equilibrium state is exact-dimensional of dimension
`min{2, dimaff(X)}`, and that `dim_H X = min{2, dimaff(X)}`. It develops the symbolic coding of
box-like sets, the thermodynamic formalism of the singular value function, the conditional
measures and entropies of Feng's Ledrappier–Young theory, and the dimension theory of the
equilibrium state and of the attractor.

[Challenge.lean](Challenge.lean) contains the seven headline results, each with an intentional
`sorry`, and their statement vocabulary over Mathlib:

| Result | Endpoint | Literature hypotheses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.2 Projections of the equilibrium state | `audit_main1` | four |
| 1.3 Hausdorff dimension equals affinity dimension | `audit_main2` | eight |
| 4.4 Dimensions of the projections | `audit_eq_state_proj_dim` | four |
| 4.16 Dimension of the projected equilibrium state | `audit_main_extended` | eight |
| 4.16, the case `s ≥ 2` | `audit_main_extended_ge_two` | none |
| Section 4.1, `dim_H X = min{2, dimaff(X)}` | `audit_dimH_eq_min` | eight |
| Section 4.1, `dimaff(X) ≥ 2` under the strong open set condition | `audit_dimH_of_two_le` | none |

[Solution.lean](Solution.lean) proves these statements from [BoxLike](BoxLike.lean). The library
and Solution are `sorry`-free and rely on Mathlib alone. The Comparator configuration permits
only the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and the library declares
no axiom.

**Coverage:** all 40 numbered statements of the manuscript have an entry with an assessment in
[paper-correspondence.yaml](paper-correspondence.yaml): 33 are formalised, 4 are theorems the
paper quotes from the literature and enter as hypotheses, Example 4.5 is formalised apart from
its numerical values, and Lemma 3.6 and Question 1.4 are intentionally not formalised. The
manifest also fingerprints 40 prose spans with the unnumbered definitions and claims.

**Literature hypotheses.** The headline results are proved conditionally on eight theorems that
the paper cites without proof. Each is a `Prop`-valued definition, stated in `Challenge.lean`
with its citation and taken as a hypothesis by the audited theorems:

- `MorrisShmerkinLyapunov`: Morris–Shmerkin, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 371 (2019), Theorem 3.1;
  the Lyapunov exponents of the equilibrium state are distinct.
- `FengKaenmakiGibbs`: Feng–Käenmäki, Discrete Contin. Dyn. Syst. 30 (2011), Proposition 1.2;
  the Gibbs bounds of Proposition 3.10.
- `FraserJordanJurgaDecomposition`: Fraser–Jordan–Jurga, J. Fractal Geom. 7 (2020), Lemma 1.3
  and Corollaries 1.5 and 1.6; the decomposition of Proposition 3.13.
- `JordanRapaportDimension`: Jordan–Rapaport, Proc. Lond. Math. Soc. 122 (2021), Theorem 1.1;
  the dimension of projected ergodic measures under exponential separation.
- `FengInfoSum`, `FengInfoSumBar`: Feng, Duke Math. J. 172 (2023), Lemma 4.6, the first identity
  of (4.16), for the planar and the projective partitions.
- `FengLedrappierYoung`, `FengLedrappierYoungBar`: Feng, Duke Math. J. 172 (2023), Theorems 1.2
  and 1.3; the Ledrappier–Young formula of Theorem 4.2, for the plane and for the projective
  system.

Theorems 1.2 and 4.4 take the first four, Theorems 1.3 and 4.16 and the dimension formula take
all eight, and the cases `s ≥ 2` and `dimaff(X) ≥ 2` take none. Off the main line, Theorem 1.1,
Proposition 2.7, the uniqueness part of Proposition 3.10 and the double-dimensionality of the
projections without separation rest on further cited theorems, also taken as hypotheses
(Fraser; Bárány–Käenmäki; Feng–Käenmäki; Feng–Hu); no audited theorem uses them. Falconer's
upper bound, which the paper also cites, is proved.

## Build and verification

Lean and Mathlib are pinned to `v4.35.0-rc2`. On a fresh independent checkout, obtain the
dependencies with `lake exe cache get`, then run:

```bash
lake build BoxLike Challenge Solution
python3 scripts/check_module_headers.py
python3 scripts/check_paper_correspondence.py
```

The seven Challenge warnings are intentional. The correspondence check needs
`../diagonal-antidiagonal.tex`; it checks that every numbered statement has an entry with its
printed number, that the fingerprints of the statements, the prose spans and the manuscript are
unchanged since the correspondence was reviewed, and that the Lean names, the comparator
configuration, `Challenge.lean`, `Solution.lean`, `formalization.yaml` and
`docs/correspondence.md` agree. It does not establish mathematical equivalence. After a change
of the manuscript has been reviewed against the Lean side, `--write-fingerprints` records the
new fingerprints. This workstation shares `.lake/packages` with other projects, so do not run
`lake update` or replace those dependencies there.

On Linux, `./comparator-audit.sh` uses the toolchain's sandboxed Comparator, Lean kernel, NanoDa
and con-ron. On macOS, `./comparator-audit.sh --local-unsandboxed` provides a local development
check with the same kernels, without Linux sandbox isolation. The
[verification workflow](.github/workflows/build.yml) builds the library and runs the Linux
audit on every push.

Submit a public repository at an immutable commit, selecting `comparator-config.json`. The
[Palomar preflight](.github/workflows/palomar-preflight.yml) runs the registry verifier on
demand. Local checks do not establish Palomar acceptance.
