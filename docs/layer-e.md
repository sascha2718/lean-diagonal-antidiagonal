# Layer E: the entropy part of `diagonal-antidiagonal.tex`

This document is the design record of the entropy layer of the library: the definitions of
`sec:lyapunov_exponents`, `sec:conditional` and `sec:entropies`, and the results of the second half
of `sec:normalisation`, `thm:feng1`, `sec:average_gibbs`, `sec:average_gibbs_entropy`,
`sec:higher_dimensional_entropies` and `sec:dimension_result_proof` that rest on them. Tex line
numbers refer to the manuscript revision fingerprinted in `paper-correspondence.yaml`.

Status: every statement of the layer is proved in the library. This includes Birkhoff's theorem,
Rokhlin's disintegration along measurable partitions, Feng's Lemma 4.2, the information-sum
identity of Feng's Lemma 4.6 (the predicates `FengInfoSum` and `FengInfoSumBar` hold:
`fengInfoSum_holds`, `fengInfoSumBar_holds`), `thm:feng_entropy_as_limit`,
`thm:feng_entropy_zero`, the Shannon–McMillan–Breiman theorem, the Kolmogorov–Sinai generator
theorem, `thm:feng_entropy_kolmogorov_sinai`, Kingman's subadditive ergodic theorem, the
Furstenberg–Kesten limits and Oseledets' theorem for invertible planar cocycles. The two
Ledrappier–Young formulas of Feng, `FengLedrappierYoung` and `FengLedrappierYoungBar`, are the
`Prop`-valued hypotheses of `BoxLike/LiteratureFeng.lean`; their transcription for every planar
self-affine set is the unauthorised proposal `PlanarFengLedrappierYoung`.

## 1. Definitions

### 1.1 Partitions as atom maps, and the domain of a partition

A partition of a set `Ω` is a map `ξ : Ω → Set Ω` sending a point to its atom. The article's
`ξ_i` and `ξ_1^⊥` are partitions of the full-measure set `Σ'` (tex lines 1893 to 1902) and
`\overline{ξ}_i` are partitions of `Σ_Z` (tex lines 1949 to 1955), while the conditional measures
are those of measures on `Σ` and on `{1, …, 2N}^ℤ` (tex line 1947: "extending it by zero").
`atomsOn D ξ` turns a partition of `D ⊆ Ω` into a partition of `Ω` by adding the single atom
`Ω ∖ D`:

```
atomsOn D ξ x = if x ∈ D then ξ x ∩ D else Dᶜ
```

Reason. `ProbabilityTheory.condExpKernel` takes a sub-`σ`-algebra of the ambient standard Borel
space. The extra atom is a null set for every measure the article conditions, so no
almost-everywhere statement changes. The atoms inside `D` are literally the article's.
`System.mem_xi_self` and `System.xi_eq_of_mem` prove that `ξ_i` is a partition of `Σ` in this
form, for every measure `μ` and every `i`.

Consequence for `thm:double_conditional` and `thm:finer_partition_dirac_mass`. The article takes
measurable partitions of a Borel set `Σ'` with `μ(Σ') = 1` and assumes `ξ'(𝐢) ⊆ ξ(𝐢)` for
`𝐢 ∈ Σ'`. The Lean statements take partitions of `Ω` with `∀ x, ξ' x ⊆ ξ x`. For the extended
partitions both atoms equal `Σ ∖ Σ'` outside `Σ'`, so the hypothesis holds everywhere;
`System.xi_succ_subset`, `System.xi_two_subset_xiPerp`, `System.xiPerp_subset_xi_zero` and
`System.xiBar_succ_subset` prove the inclusions of tex lines 1908 to 1910 and 1960 in this form.

### 1.2 The `σ`-algebra `\widehat{ξ}`

`saturated ξ` is the `σ`-algebra of Borel sets `A` with `x ∈ A ↔ y ∈ A` whenever `y ∈ ξ x`. For
a partition these are the Borel unions of atoms, which is Feng's definition (Section 2.1: "the
sub-`σ`-algebra of `B` whose sets are `ξ`-saturated"). The symmetric form of the condition makes
`saturated ξ` a `σ`-algebra for every map `ξ`, and `saturated ξ ≤ mΩ` holds by construction
(`saturated_le`), so the kernel of 1.4 is formed without any measurability proof.

The article writes `\widehat{ξ}` for "the `σ`-algebra generated by `ξ`" (tex line 1792). When the
atoms are the fibres of a Borel map between standard Borel spaces, the saturated Borel sets and
the pull-back of the Borel `σ`-algebra under the inducing map are equal, exactly and not only
modulo null sets (`saturated_eq_comap`). The proof is Blackwell's theorem through Lusin separation
(`MeasurableSet.analyticSet_image`, `AnalyticSet.measurablySeparable`).

`IsMeasurablePartition ξ` says that the atoms are the fibres of a Borel map `f : Ω → ℝ`:
`∀ x y, y ∈ ξ x ↔ f y = f x`. Every standard Borel space embeds measurably into `ℝ`
(`MeasureTheory.embeddingReal`), so the target `ℝ` loses nothing and avoids a universe variable.

### 1.3 Instances

`condExpKernel` needs `[StandardBorelSpace Ω]` and `[IsFiniteMeasure μ]`. For the alphabet the
library's standing instances are `[Fintype ι] [MeasurableSpace ι] [MeasurableSingletonClass ι]`.
They give `DiscreteMeasurableSpace ι` (`MeasurableSingletonClass.toDiscreteMeasurableSpace`),
hence `StandardBorelSpace ι` (`standardBorelSpace_of_discreteMeasurableSpace`), hence
`StandardBorelSpace (ℤ → ι)` and `StandardBorelSpace (ℤ → ι × ZMod 2)`
(`StandardBorelSpace.pi_countable`, `StandardBorelSpace.prod`). Two `example`s in
`BoxLike/Conditional.lean` check both by `inferInstance`. `condMeas μ ξ x` is a probability
measure (an instance in `BoxLike/Conditional.lean`), `μ[|Ξ]` is finite by Mathlib, and
`avgMeasure μ₁ μ₂` is finite and a probability measure (`isProbabilityMeasure_avgMeasure`).

### 1.4 Conditional measures

`condMeas μ ξ x = condExpKernel μ (saturated ξ) x` mirrors Rokhlin's conditional measures of tex
lines 1783 to 1797. The kernel is defined at every point, is `\widehat{ξ}`-measurable
(`measurable_condMeas`), satisfies `∫_B μ_x^ξ(A) dμ = μ(A ∩ B)` (`setLIntegral_condMeas`) and is
determined up to a `μ`-null set of points (`condMeas_unique`).

"Supported on `ξ(𝐢)`" (tex line 1785) has no pointwise meaning for a kernel defined almost
everywhere. Its meaning here is the statement `condMeas_atom`: for a measurable partition,
`μ_x^ξ(ξ(x)) = 1` for `μ`-almost every `x`.

Identities between conditional measures are almost-everywhere equalities of measures. The
article's "simultaneously for all Borel sets `A`" (tex line 1814) is the equality of the two
measures. Where the article writes `μ({𝐢 : μ_𝐢^ξ = δ_𝐢}) = 1`, the Lean statement is
`∀ᵐ x ∂μ, condMeas μ ξ x = Measure.dirac x`; this needs no measurability of the set.

The normalised restriction `μ_Ξ` (tex lines 1800 to 1805) is Mathlib's `ProbabilityTheory.cond`,
written `μ[|Ξ]`.

### 1.5 Conditional information and the entropies

- `condInfo μ ξ x = -log μ_x^ξ([x_0])` is `I_μ(𝒫 | \widehat{ξ})(x)` of tex lines 1764 to 1769,
  with the conditional expectation `E_μ(1_{[x_0]} | \widehat{ξ})` realised by the kernel. It is
  non-negative (`condInfo_nonneg`). Where `μ_x^ξ([x_0]) = 0`, Lean's `Real.log 0 = 0` gives `0`
  and the article `+∞`; this set is `μ`-null (Feng, Lemma 2.2).
- `condEntropy μ ξ = ∫ condInfo μ ξ dμ` is `H_μ(𝒫 | \widehat{ξ})` (tex lines 1770 to 1774). The
  integral is a genuine value by `integrable_condInfo` (Feng, (4.15); tex lines 1933 to 1934). For
  countable partitions and arbitrary sub-`σ`-algebras the general objects are in
  `BoxLike/Support/ConditionalInformation.lean`.
- `localEntropy μ ξ = μ[condInfo μ ξ | MeasurableSpace.invariants (shift 1)]` is
  `E_μ(I_μ(𝒫 | \widehat{ξ}) | ℐ)` (tex lines 1920 to 1925). Mathlib's `invariants` is the
  `σ`-algebra of Borel sets with `σ⁻¹A = A`, which is the article's `ℐ`. The function is a
  conditional expectation and is determined up to a null set, so every statement about
  `h_i(μ, 𝐢)` is an almost-everywhere statement, as in the article.

How `h_i(μ)` is obtained from `h_i(μ, 𝐢)`. The article defines
`h_i(μ) = ∫ h_i(μ, 𝐢) dμ = H_μ(𝒫 | \widehat{ξ}_i)` (tex lines 1926 to 1932). Lean defines `h_i(μ)`
as `H_μ(𝒫 | \widehat{ξ}_i)`; `integral_localEntropy` proves the first equality, and
`localEntropy_ae_eq` states `eq:ergodic_entropy_constant`.

`h_0` and the Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy. The library's `entropy μ` is
`lim_n n⁻¹ H_μ(𝒫_0^{n-1})`; `ksEntropy` is the supremum over finite Borel partitions of tex lines
2000 to 2007, and the generator theorem `ksEntropy_shift_eq_entropy` identifies the two.
`thm:feng1` carries `h_0(μ) = H_μ(𝒫 | \widehat{ξ}_0)`. The bridges are
`System.dimEntropy_zero_eq_entropy`, `System.dimEntropyBar_zero_eq_entropy` (entropy rate, by the
chain rule, Lévy's upward theorem and Cesàro averaging) and `System.dimEntropy_zero_eq_ksEntropy`,
`System.dimEntropyBar_zero_eq_ksEntropy` (tex lines 2024 to 2026). Hypotheses such as
`h_0(μ) ≤ -λ_1(𝖠, μ)` are written with `entropy μ`, which is the quantity in `eq:eq_state_def`
and in the real-variable lemmas of `LyapunovDim`.

Two-sided shifts are used throughout, as in the article and in Feng: the partitions fix the past
`𝐢^-` and `π` depends on the future `𝐢^+`. `SamePast x y` is `∀ n < 0, x n = y n` (tex lines 844
to 845). `𝒫_0^{n-1}(𝐢)` is `cylinder (block n x)`.

### 1.6 `Σ'`, `Σ^τ`, `Σ^ω`

The article takes `Σ'` from Oseledets' theorem (tex lines 1620 to 1642): an invariant Borel set
of full measure carrying the filtration with (V1) to (V3), chosen saturated with respect to the
past (tex lines 1652 to 1653), and defines `Σ^τ`, `Σ^ω` by the position of `V_𝐢^1`
(`eq:sigma_tau_omega`). The library has three representations, which agree.

- The rate-defined box-like sets of `BoxLike/SlowAxis.lean`, used by the main line:

  ```
  S.HasBackRate y l  :=  Tendsto (fun n => log |S.gDeriv (pastWord y n)| / n) atTop (𝓝 l)
  S.SigmaTau μ   = {x | HasBackRate (τ x) λ₁ ∧ HasBackRate (ω x) λ₂}     -- V¹ is the y-axis
  S.SigmaOmega μ = {x | HasBackRate (τ x) λ₂ ∧ HasBackRate (ω x) λ₁}     -- V¹ is the x-axis
  S.SigmaPrime μ = S.SigmaTau μ ∪ S.SigmaOmega μ
  ```

  By `thm:matrix_products_g_derivatives` the rate along `τ(𝐢)` is the growth rate of
  `|A_{i_{-n}} ⋯ A_{i_{-1}} e_1|` and the rate along `ω(𝐢)` that of `e_2`
  (`System.hasBackRate_tau_iff`, `System.hasBackRate_omega_iff`). The sets are Borel
  (`measurableSet_SigmaTau`, `measurableSet_SigmaOmega`, `measurableSet_SigmaPrime`), saturated
  with respect to the past (`mem_SigmaTau_of_samePast`, `mem_SigmaOmega_of_samePast`), disjoint
  when `λ_2 < λ_1` (`disjoint_SigmaTau_SigmaOmega`, `eq:tau_omega_partition2`), of full measure for
  ergodic `μ` (`measure_SigmaPrime_compl`, from Birkhoff's theorem for the lift
  `(τ_*μ + ω_*μ)/2`), and shift invariant (`shift_mem_SigmaTau_iff`, which is
  `eq:sigma_tau_omega_positive_eq_measure1`).
- The article's choice, `MatrixTuple.OseledetsSet` of `BoxLike/OseledetsFiltration.lean`: a Borel,
  invariant, past-saturated set of full measure with the filtration and (V1) to (V3). For a
  box-like tuple every choice has `V_𝐢^1` a coordinate axis (`System.V_one_eq_axis`), the box-like
  set with the coordinate filtration is a choice (`System.oseledetsSet`), and the article's
  `Σ^τ`, `Σ^ω` agree with the rate-defined ones modulo null sets (`System.sigmaTau_ae_eq`,
  `System.sigmaOmega_ae_eq`).
- The set of regular points of an arbitrary invertible planar tuple, `MatrixTuple.regularSet` of
  `BoxLike/Planar/Oseledets.lean`, the largest set on which (V1) to (V3) hold, with the slow line
  given by `eq:V_lyapunov`. It is Borel, invariant and past-saturated; it has full measure for
  ergodic `μ` with `λ_2 < λ_1` by Oseledets' theorem
  (`MatrixTuple.measure_regularSet_compl_of_ergodic`); for a box-like system it is the box-like
  `Σ'` (`System.toPlanar_SigmaPrime`).

### 1.7 The slow axis and `V_𝐢^i`

`S.slowAxis μ x : ZMod 2` is `1` on `Σ^τ` (the `y`-axis) and `0` elsewhere, which on `Σ^ω` is
the `x`-axis when `λ_2 < λ_1`. `S.oseledets μ x i` is `ℝ²` for `i = 0`, the axis
`{v | coord (slowAxis + 1) v = 0}` for `i = 1` and `{0}` for `i ≥ 2` (`mem_oseledets_iff`). The
slow axis depends only on the past (`slowAxis_eq_of_samePast`), which is Feng's Lemma 4.1 in the
box-like case. For a planar system `PlanarSystem.oseledets` is `MatrixTuple.filtration`, the
slow direction is spanned by `(cos θ, sin θ)` (`MatrixTuple.slowLine_eq_cross`), and (V1) is
`MatrixTuple.image_slowLine`.

### 1.8 The partitions

```
S.xi μ i    = atomsOn Σ'  fun x => {y | SamePast y x ∧ π y - π x ∈ V_x^i}
S.xiPerp μ  = atomsOn Σ'  fun x => {y | SamePast y x ∧ coord (slowAxis x) (π y - π x) = 0}
S.xiBar 0   = atomsOn Σ_Z fun y => {z | SamePast z y}
S.xiBar _   = atomsOn Σ_Z fun y => {z | SamePast z y ∧ \overline{π} z = \overline{π} y}
```

These are tex lines 1893 to 1902 and 1949 to 1955 verbatim. Orthogonality to a coordinate axis
is the vanishing of that coordinate. Indices above the article's range repeat the last partition.
`PlanarSystem.xi`, `PlanarSystem.xiPerp` are the same definitions for a planar system, and agree
with the box-like ones pointwise for `S.toPlanar` (`System.toPlanar_xi`, `System.toPlanar_xiPerp`).
Feng's partitions on his admissible set and the article's partitions on a choice of `Σ'` have
almost everywhere the same conditional measures (`System.condMeas_fengXi_ae_eq`,
`System.condMeas_oseledetsXi_ae_eq`, `PlanarSystem.condMeas_fengXi_ae_eq`).

### 1.9 Entropies of the system

`S.dimEntropy μ i`, `S.dimEntropyAt μ i`, `S.dimEntropyBar ν i`, `S.dimEntropyBarAt ν i` are
`h_i(μ)`, `h_i(μ, ·)`, `\overline{h}_i(ν)`, `\overline{h}_i(ν, ·)`; `PlanarSystem.dimEntropy` and
`PlanarSystem.dimEntropyAt` are those of a planar system.

### 1.10 The average measure, the Gibbs measures, the separation condition

`avgMeasure μ₁ μ₂ = (2⁻¹ : ℝ≥0) • (μ₁ + μ₂)` is `eq:average_gibbs`. The scalar lies in `ℝ≥0`
because Mathlib's finiteness instance for scalar multiples is for `ℝ≥0`.

"`μ_1`, `μ_2` are the `s`-Gibbs measures" is the hypothesis
`hdec : S.GibbsDecomposition s μ μ₁ μ₂`, the conclusion of `thm:eq_state_is_sum`, together with
`[IsProbabilityMeasure μ₁] [IsProbabilityMeasure μ₂]` where conditional measures of `μ_k` occur.
`S.fastPart μ = τ(Σ^τ) ∪ ω(Σ^ω)` and `S.slowPart μ = ω(Σ^τ) ∪ τ(Σ^ω)` name the two unions of
`thm:gibbs_tau_omega_positive`. The inverses `\overline{τ}`, `\overline{ω}` are `unCode`, and
`\overline{τ}(B)` for `B ⊆ τ(Σ)` is `unCode '' B`. The strong open set condition is
`System.StrongOpenSet` of `BoxLike/SeparationConditions.lean`.

### 1.11 Hypotheses

The statements take the article's hypotheses: `S.WithRotations`, `S.Proximal` or, where the
article assumes the normalised tuple, `S.HasProximalDiagonal`; `S.ProjExpSeparation`; `0 < s`,
`s < 2`; `[IsProbabilityMeasure μ] (hinv : MeasurePreserving (shift 1) μ μ)`;
`S.IsEquilibriumState s μ`; `hP : S.pressure s = 0` for `s = dimaff(𝖠)`. Ergodicity of the
equilibrium state is the hypothesis `herg : Ergodic (shift 1) μ` in the lemmas, and a theorem in
the assembly (`System.ergodic_of_hasProximalDiagonal`). The statements hold for every invariant
equilibrium state; uniqueness is not used. `S.lyapunov₂ μ < S.lyapunov₁ μ` is a hypothesis `hlt`
where the article assumes it and is derived from `MorrisShmerkinLyapunov` through
`System.simple_lyapunov` in the assembly. The cited theorems enter as the hypotheses `hms`, `hfk`,
`hfjj`, `hjr`, `hly`, `hlyb` of `docs/correspondence.md`, "Fidelity boundary"; the binders `hfi`,
`hfib` of the library statements receive the theorems `fengInfoSum_holds`,
`fengInfoSumBar_holds`.

## 4. Literature and standard theorems

Under the rules of `docs/correspondence.md`, "Fidelity boundary", a cited theorem is a
`Prop`-valued definition that the results using it take as a hypothesis, and new ones need
explicit authorisation. For this layer:

- `FengLedrappierYoung`, `FengLedrappierYoungBar`: D.-J. Feng, *Dimension of invariant measures
  for affine iterated function systems*, Duke Math. J. 172 (2023), Theorems 1.2 and 1.3
  (arXiv:1901.01691v2). The planar statement is the case `d = 2`, `s = 2`, `k_1 = k_2 = 1`; the
  scalar statement is the case `d = 1` for `Ψ`. Feng's `Σ'` can be taken inside the box-like `Σ'`
  and his partitions have almost everywhere the same conditional measures as the library's
  (`System.condMeas_fengXi_ae_eq`). For `Ψ` the article restricts `\overline{ξ}_i` to `Σ_Z`, which
  has full measure (tex line 1947). Degenerate instance checked by hand for both parts of
  `thm:feng1`: a Dirac mass on a constant sequence over a diagonal generator with `|a| ≠ |b|`
  gives `h_i = 0`, the formula gives `0`, and the projected measure is a Dirac mass of pointwise
  dimension `0`. For `λ_1 = λ_2` the planar statement is silent through `hlt`.
- Proved standard theorems: Birkhoff, Maker, Shannon–McMillan–Breiman, the Kolmogorov–Sinai
  generator theorem, Kingman, Furstenberg–Kesten, Oseledets for invertible planar cocycles,
  Rokhlin's disintegration, Feng's Lemmas 2.1(v), 4.2, 4.4, 4.5 and 4.6. Mathlib supplies the
  martingale convergence theorems (`MeasureTheory.Integrable.tendsto_ae_condExp`), the
  disintegration kernel (`condExpKernel`), its uniqueness
  (`Mathlib/Probability/Kernel/Disintegration/Unique.lean`), Lusin–Souslin
  (`MeasurableSet.image_of_measurable_injOn`) and Lusin separation.
- Morris–Shmerkin, Fraser–Jordan–Jurga, Feng–Käenmäki, Jordan–Rapaport: the `Prop`s
  `MorrisShmerkinLyapunov`, `FraserJordanJurgaDecomposition`, `FengKaenmakiGibbs` and
  `JordanRapaportDimension` of `BoxLike/Literature.lean`.
- Feng–Hu, Theorem 2.8 (tex line 2880): contained in `JordanRapaportDimension` under separation;
  the `Prop` `FengHuExactDim` of `BoxLike/LiteratureRemarks.lean` serves the remark without
  separation.
- Rossi, Theorem 2.2 (`eq:hausdorff_ergodic_lyapunov`): taken by no theorem, since the proof of
  `thm:main_extended` evaluates Feng's formula to an equality.
- Falconer's bound `eq:hausdorff_affinity_upper_bound`: proved for every planar self-affine set.
- `eq:dimension_measure_def`: proved (`System.le_dimH_attractor` and `LocalDimBounds`).
